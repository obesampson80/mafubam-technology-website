# Human-First Anti-Generic Standards

This document defines how the MAFUBAM website must avoid looking like a generic AI-generated website.

## 1. Core Rule

Every major section should contain at least one detail that could only reasonably belong to MAFUBAM, Port Harcourt, the founder's real work, the company's registered identity, or the actual service process.

If a section could be copied unchanged onto another technology company's website, rewrite or redesign it.

## 2. What Makes a Website Look AI-Generated

Avoid these patterns:

- Generic hero lines such as "Empowering businesses with innovative digital solutions."
- Abstract 3D shapes, glowing grids, random code backgrounds and floating gradient blobs.
- Repeated card grids where every card has the same length and rhythm.
- Vague claims such as "seamless", "cutting-edge", "world-class", "revolutionary" and "next-generation".
- Overly perfect spacing with no local, personal or operational detail.
- Service copy that explains categories but not actual deliverables.
- Testimonials without names, context, project type or believable specificity.
- Portfolio placeholders with no before/after, problem, approach or result.
- Large hero sections that say very little.
- AI-style paragraphs that explain obvious things without helping the visitor decide.

## 3. What Makes the Site Feel Human

Use these patterns:

- Specific Port Harcourt and Nigeria context.
- Founder-led wording where appropriate.
- Real service workflows.
- Real project screenshots and document samples.
- Before-and-after examples.
- Clear boundaries around ethics, scope, payment and revisions.
- Practical language based on how clients actually ask for help.
- Small operational details: file types, delivery formats, consultation style, turnaround rules.
- Direct WhatsApp enquiry paths with prefilled, natural messages.
- Plain explanations of what MAFUBAM does and does not do.

## 4. Visual Identity Rules

The site should look designed, not templated.

Use:

- A distinctive but restrained layout rhythm.
- Real screenshots, diagrams, documents and charts as visual proof.
- Carefully selected local or work-related photography if real assets are available.
- Subtle texture only if it supports the brand and does not reduce readability.
- Section compositions that vary naturally while staying consistent.
- Compact visual proof near claims.

Avoid:

- Generic laptop-on-desk stock photos.
- Random AI-generated "tech office" images.
- Overused blue/purple gradients.
- Bento grids used only for decoration.
- Overly rounded glass cards.
- Fake dashboards that do not represent actual services.
- Heavy animation used to create excitement instead of clarity.

## 5. Copywriting Rules

Write like a real service provider speaking to a real client.

Use:

- "Send your dataset, instructions and deadline."
- "We review the files first, then issue a written quote."
- "We can format your document, check references and prepare Word/PDF files."
- "We do not fabricate data, impersonate students or guarantee academic outcomes."
- "For websites, domain, hosting and content are quoted separately unless stated."

Avoid:

- "We leverage innovative digital transformation solutions."
- "Unlock your potential with our cutting-edge ecosystem."
- "Our team empowers success through seamless technology."
- "Transform your vision into reality."
- "Your one-stop solution for everything digital."

## 6. Section-Level Specificity Test

Before approving any page section, ask:

- Does this section mention a real service outcome?
- Does it tell the visitor what to do next?
- Does it include a concrete detail, example, process step or boundary?
- Would this still make sense if copied to another tech company site?
- Does the language sound like a real person from the business would say it?

If the section fails two or more of these questions, revise it.

## 7. Homepage Requirements

The homepage must not open with a generic startup hero.

Required first-screen signals:

- MAFUBAM Technology or MAFUBAM TECHNOLOGY LTD.
- Digital Solutions for Research and Business.
- Port Harcourt and remote service signal.
- Two audience paths: Research Solutions and Business Technology Solutions.
- Primary CTA: Request a Quote.
- Secondary CTA: Chat on WhatsApp.

The hero should include a practical visual:

- A real document/report preview,
- a chart/dashboard preview,
- a technical diagram preview,
- or a website/project preview.

Do not use a vague abstract tech illustration as the hero proof.

## 8. Service Page Requirements

Each service page should include:

- Who it is for.
- What problem it solves.
- What the client sends.
- What MAFUBAM delivers.
- What is excluded.
- How pricing is determined.
- How revisions work.
- CTA to request a quote.

This structure makes the page more useful and less generic.

## 9. Portfolio Requirements

Portfolio content must be evidence-based.

Each portfolio item should include:

- Project category.
- Client type or anonymised context.
- The original problem.
- What MAFUBAM did.
- The final output.
- A visual sample.
- Confidentiality note if needed.

Do not use fake case studies. If real client work cannot be shown yet, label samples honestly as "sample project", "concept redesign" or "demonstration output".

## 10. Human Details To Collect

Collect these before visual design:

- Founder name and preferred public profile style.
- Founder short bio in plain language.
- Real location wording: Port Harcourt, Rivers State, Nigeria.
- WhatsApp number and business hours.
- Services the founder most enjoys or delivers strongest.
- Past project types that can be mentioned.
- Common client questions already received.
- Actual file formats handled: Word, PDF, Excel, SPSS, Python notebooks, PowerPoint, PNG, SVG, etc.
- Actual tools used where useful: Excel, Python, SPSS, Word, PowerPoint, Figma, diagrams.net, VS Code, etc.
- Real delivery policies: deposit, revisions, urgent work and final-file release.

## 11. Human-First Design Research Notes

Current design guidance points toward human-centered, distinctive and trust-led websites. Useful patterns include concise content, warmer brand expression, real proof, authentic visual assets and purposeful motion.

Research references:

- Nielsen Norman Group: users scan pages and need concise, structured web writing.
- Nielsen Norman Group: animation should support comprehension, not distract.
- WCAG 2.2: accessibility must be planned from the start.
- DataReportal Nigeria 2026: Nigeria is mobile-first, so performance and WhatsApp-first flows matter.
- Current 2026 design trend reports: brands are moving away from sterile, generic AI-like layouts toward human warmth, texture, real people, real proof and more distinctive brand expression.
