# User Taste and Audience Fit

Prepared: August 2026

This document defines how the MAFUBAM website should match the taste, expectations and decision style of its real users.

## 1. Core Principle

The website must not be designed only for visual awards or designer taste. It must feel right to the people who will actually use it:

- Nigerian mobile users
- Students and postgraduate researchers
- Lecturers and academic consultants
- Small business owners
- Schools, churches, clinics, hotels and local organisations
- Professionals looking for practical digital help

The site should feel modern, but not strange. It should feel professional, but not cold. It should feel technical, but not confusing.

## 2. Shared User Expectations

Most visitors will want to know quickly:

- What does MAFUBAM do?
- Is this for my kind of problem?
- Can I trust this company with my files or business?
- How much might it cost?
- How fast can they deliver?
- Can I contact them on WhatsApp?

Design implication:

- Show services, proof, trust and WhatsApp access early.
- Keep wording direct and practical.
- Do not hide important contact or pricing guidance behind too many clicks.

## 3. Nigerian User Taste

Current Nigerian web expectations strongly favor:

- Mobile-first design
- Fast loading on mobile data
- WhatsApp contact
- Clear pricing or quote guidance
- Visible business legitimacy
- Local context
- Dark mode or dark-mode comfort
- Simple navigation
- Practical proof

Design implication:

- The mobile version is the main product experience, not a secondary version.
- WhatsApp should be visible but not aggressive.
- Contact details should appear in header/footer and on the contact page.
- CAC registration and Port Harcourt location should support trust.

## 4. Research and Academic Audience Taste

This audience values:

- Clarity
- Confidentiality
- Formatting quality
- Technical competence
- Data-analysis credibility
- Ethical boundaries
- Ability to meet deadlines
- Clear instructions on what to send

They may be anxious about:

- Cost
- Deadline
- Whether their work will be exposed
- Whether the provider understands academic formats
- Whether support is legitimate

Design implication:

- Use calm design, not flashy marketing.
- Show document, chart, diagram and slide samples.
- Explain confidentiality and ethical support clearly.
- Use service labels they understand: data analysis, formatting, diagrams, defence slides.

## 5. Business and Organisation Audience Taste

This audience values:

- Professional appearance
- Business credibility
- Clear process
- Practical outcomes
- Website/mobile quality
- Maintenance and support
- Response speed
- Local availability

They may be skeptical of:

- Overly academic positioning
- Too much technical jargon
- Informal freelancer language
- Websites that look like templates

Design implication:

- Business service pages should look operational and polished.
- Use proof of websites, dashboards, forms and professional documents.
- Keep research-heavy wording away from the Business Technology page.
- Show what is included/excluded in website work.

## 6. Dark Mode Taste

Dark mode can suit modern Nigerian users and technical audiences, but it must be implemented carefully.

Recommended approach:

- Use a dark-mode option or dark sections.
- Keep proof visuals like `hero-proof-workbench-v2@2x.png` as bright framed evidence panels.
- Do not invert or darken the generated proof image.
- Use navy/charcoal backgrounds with light text and restrained teal accents.
- Maintain strong contrast.

Best treatment:

- Dark navy page background
- Light proof image inside a framed surface
- Soft shadow or thin border
- Clear white/light-grey text
- WhatsApp accent used sparingly

Avoid:

- Pure black everywhere
- Neon cyberpunk styling
- Purple-heavy AI startup gradients
- Low-contrast grey text
- Inverting screenshots and charts

## 7. Light Mode Taste

Light mode should remain the default or primary experience unless a final design decision says otherwise.

Recommended approach:

- Light backgrounds for content-heavy pages.
- Deep navy headings.
- Teal/cyan accents.
- White cards or lightly tinted sections.
- Strong proof visuals.

Why:

- Research and business content is easier to read.
- Documents/charts look natural on light surfaces.
- It feels professional and accessible.

## 8. Recommended Theme Strategy

Best option:

- Build a polished light-first website with dark-mode support.

If only one theme is built first:

- Use light mode as default, but design dark sections and dark-compatible components from the start.

Reason:

- Light mode is safer for document-heavy content.
- Dark mode helps modern taste, night browsing and technical feel.
- The site should not depend on dark mode to look modern.

## 9. Taste-Fit Rules For UI Design

Use:

- Real proof visuals
- Clean typography
- Local trust signals
- Clear service categories
- WhatsApp-first enquiry
- Modest animation
- Human copy
- Fast mobile layout
- Proof before claims

Avoid:

- Generic AI hero copy
- Abstract tech decoration
- Overly playful colors
- Heavy dark gradients
- Too many cards
- Aggressive popups
- Huge empty hero sections
- Copy that sounds foreign to Nigerian users

## 10. Taste-Fit Test

Before approving any design, ask:

- Would a postgraduate student trust this site with a document or dataset?
- Would a Port Harcourt SME owner trust this company with a website?
- Is WhatsApp easy to find?
- Does the site look real within 10 seconds?
- Does it feel modern without looking like a generic AI template?
- Does it load and read well on a small phone?
- Are services separated clearly enough for both audiences?
- Is there proof near every major claim?

If the answer is no to any major question, revise before coding.
