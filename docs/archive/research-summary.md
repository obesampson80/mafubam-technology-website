# Research Summary

Prepared: August 2026

## 1. Website Type Decision

The first MAFUBAM website should be a static business website, not a full web application.

The blueprint requires a sales and trust platform with service pages, proof, package guidance, contact details, WhatsApp calls to action and a request-a-quote flow. These can be delivered without a backend.

Backend features should be delayed until there is clear demand for database storage, client accounts, file uploads, online payments, automated invoices or an internal CRM.

## 2. Nigerian Digital Context

Nigeria is a mobile-first market. DataReportal's Digital 2026 Nigeria report states that Nigeria had 109 million internet users at the end of 2025, with internet penetration at 45.5 percent. It also reports 165 million cellular mobile connections, equivalent to 69.2 percent of the population.

Implications:

- The site must be fast on mobile networks.
- Pages should be light, compressed and readable.
- WhatsApp should be treated as a primary conversion channel, not an afterthought.
- The site should avoid heavy animation, large video backgrounds and unnecessary scripts.

Source: https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-nigeria

## 3. Local Competitor Observations

Several Port Harcourt technology competitors position around website design, software development, IT consulting, data analytics, training, cybersecurity, cloud computing and digital transformation.

Observed competitors and references:

- Olive Microsystems: web development, mobile apps, training and consulting in Port Harcourt.
- MEL Technologies: software development, website design, data analytics, cybersecurity, cloud computing and IT training.
- TideRelay Technologies: clear service pages, fixed-quote language, websites, software, SEO and local Port Harcourt positioning.
- Vision360 Technology: website design, digital marketing, IT training and strong local address visibility.
- Grey InfoTech: web design, mobile apps, UX/UI, digital strategy and process-led language.

Implications:

- MAFUBAM should not present itself as just another generic "web design company".
- The strongest differentiation is the combination of research support, data analysis, documentation, diagrams, websites and business technology.
- The site should separate Research Solutions and Business Technology Solutions clearly, so each audience feels directly addressed.
- Local credibility matters: Port Harcourt location, WhatsApp, phone, business hours and visible process should be easy to find.

## 4. Research-Service Competitor Observations

Academic and research-service sites often emphasize low prices, speed, WhatsApp ordering, data analysis, Chapter 4 interpretation, formatting, revisions and confidentiality.

Observed patterns:

- AlimsWrite uses clear service pages, starting prices, request forms and sample output language.
- Avalanche Consult groups services into academic and business categories with package cards and WhatsApp calls to action.
- Cee Writing emphasizes research analytics, confidentiality, WhatsApp orders and revision promises.

Risks:

- Many academic-service websites can appear to offer full project writing or unethical academic outsourcing.
- MAFUBAM should avoid claims that imply impersonation, guaranteed grades, fabricated results or complete academic work on behalf of students.

Implications:

- Use "ethical research support" as a trust signal.
- Offer formatting, analysis, interpretation guidance, visualisation, diagrams, presentation design and programming support.
- Avoid wording such as "we write your full thesis" or "ready to submit" unless framed as formatting/editing of client-owned work.

## 5. UX and Conversion Principles

Useful UX guidance from Nielsen Norman Group:

- Users scan web pages, so content should be short, structured and easy to compare.
- Competitive usability evaluation helps identify what competitors do well or poorly before design begins.
- List and card entries should have consistent structure so users can compare options quickly.
- Animation should be used sparingly and only when it helps comprehension.
- Research can begin before a product exists by studying user needs, competitor behavior and real workflows.

Implications:

- Use concise sections, strong headings and direct service labels.
- Make service cards consistent: outcome, audience, deliverables, starting price or "from quote", CTA.
- Avoid large blocks of text on the homepage.
- Use motion only for small feedback, hover states or reveal interactions.
- Prioritize enquiry clarity over visual novelty.

## 6. Accessibility Requirements

WCAG 2.2 guidance should shape the website from the start.

Important points:

- Text contrast should meet at least WCAG AA contrast expectations.
- Touch targets should be large enough for mobile use.
- Focus states should be visible.
- Forms need clear labels, helpful errors and no unnecessary repeated entry.
- Navigation and help patterns should stay consistent across pages.
- The design should not rely on color alone to communicate meaning.

Sources:

- https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/
- https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag2mobile-22/

## 7. Current Design Direction

Current professional service-site trends that fit MAFUBAM:

- Clean editorial layouts with strong typography.
- Split audience pathways instead of one long undifferentiated service list.
- Real proof: samples, case studies, screenshots, testimonials and process.
- Compact service comparison cards.
- Sticky or persistent WhatsApp/contact access on mobile.
- Calm color systems with one strong accent.
- Fast, accessible pages instead of heavy visual effects.
- Clear "how we work" process sections.
- Pricing shown as starting ranges or scoped quote guidance.

Trends to avoid:

- Generic AI-generated tech illustrations as the main proof.
- Overused dark-blue gradient pages without real service evidence.
- Overly animated hero sections.
- Vague claims such as "best", "number one" or "world-class" without proof.
- Mixing student/research messages with business-client messages on the same service detail page.
